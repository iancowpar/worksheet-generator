Case Study 01

The AI
Capability Layer

A peer community of twenty PMs. Built from zero. No top-down mandate.

The mechanism

Shared infrastructure. Peer community. On-ramp ritual.

The adoption arc

From one workflow to twenty PMs running their own.

Seed 1 Personal productivity OS. Twenty slash commands in active use.
Founding peer group 3–5 Working cadence. Honest about what wasn't working. No decks.
Community 20 Custom libraries. Champions emerging without being assigned.

First-command stickiness. Champion emergence. Workflow extinction. Each PM’s first slash command was the conversion mechanism; the second one was the proof they were inside the community.

The outcomes
20 PMs

Twenty custom slash-command libraries. Multiple PMs became champions without being assigned. Workflows retired, not workflows launched.

The measurement principle

Adoption measured by what stopped, not what launched.

Extinction over addition. The only adoption that lasts is the kind nobody had to order.

Case Study 02

The UTA
Operating System

$250M ARR · 300 customers · one weekly decision.

The mechanism

Three components, one operating system.

01 Composite ARR-risk model Top accounts scored on weighted exposure: ARR, case volume, true-defect rate, sentiment. Replaced raw ticket counts with weighted signal.
02 Five-panel analytics product Opinionated by design. An embedded Claude Code function as the routing layer surfaced the single highest-leverage action across the panels.
03 Weekly reporting cadence Automated. Delivered to the program lead every Monday morning. Ranked list of what to look at this week and why.
The signal routing

Five panels in. One ranked action out.

The outcomes
~$50M

in at-risk ARR surfaced before escalation. Working defect list cut ~97%. A weekly cadence the program lead actually acted on.

The measurement principle

The system’s value was measured by whether decisions changed.

Not whether the dashboard had users. Not whether adoption metrics were green. Whether the program lead made different calls than they would have without the tool.
