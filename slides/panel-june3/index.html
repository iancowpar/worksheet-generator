Case Study 01

The AI
Capability Layer

Six weeks. From one PM to twenty.
No top-down mandate.

The catalyst · March 2026

“Wait. How long would it take you to write me one of these?”

Vasu, product partner, after his first slash command. The moment one PM’s tool became a question another PM asked.

The infrastructure

Four pieces. Small enough to hold on one screen.

01 Slash command library Commands for workflows people hated. Eight to ten by week two. Each one a small thing that used to be manual and now wasn’t.
02 Confluence dashboards Built with Claude Code. Epic health, retro action items, roadmap state. Became how the team talked about its own work.
03 The build board One Confluence page. Index of every dashboard and command. A map of the work small enough to share in one screen.
04 GitHub repo Commands and templates as shared substrate. A README that oriented a new PM in five minutes.
The adoption shapes

Three different ways the work spread.

DIRECT ENABLEMENT Maria · Translation We sat together for two hours and wrote a command that cloned a language deployment across five teams. She retired her old workflow that afternoon.
OBSERVED & ADOPTED Laura · Ascentis Watched me automate my QBR. About four hours of work collapsed to ten minutes. She took the command and ran her own QBR at the end of March.
PEER PROPAGATION Keith & Vasu Wrote their own commands. Contributed back to the shared repo. The WFM Slack channel became a two-way exchange, not a one-way feed.
The moment that told me it was working

“It’s magic. It feels like life will never be the same again.”

Maria · Translation team · the morning she retired her language-deployment workflow.

The measurement principle

Adoption measured by what stopped, not what launched.

Three named workflows retired across three teams in six weeks. Peers building and contributing without being asked. The honest measure was whether it kept running when I wasn’t running it.

Case Study 02

The UTA
Upgrade Program

300 customers · $250M ARR · a tech-stack cliff edge by November 2026.

The operating mechanism

Four sources in. One ranked list of risk out.

INPUT Four data sources Sentiment from CSM leads and Salesforce.
Defect counts from Jira.
Case volume from Salesforce.
ARR exposure from Salesforce + Power BI.
CLASSIFICATION Jira label taxonomy Co-designed with Leila, our engineering manager. Customer config issues. Pre-existing issues. Only code fixes requiring deployment counted as defects.
OUTPUT Weighted top-5 list ARR-dominant. True-defect-dominant. Reviewed daily in standup. Reviewed weekly in the cross-functional call.
The negotiation that mattered most

Pod-based scheduling versus complexity-based.

ENGINEERING’S PREFERENCE By database pod Operationally efficient.
Predictable sprint capacity.
Clean batching of work.
PRODUCT’S COUNTER By complexity Operationally resilient.
Customer delays stay local.
The cadence survives reality.

Complexity won. Not because product had more power. Because the delivery date was the load-bearing constraint, and only one of the two approaches survived the first customer asking us to hold off.

The hardest test case

A large university. Five flags lit at once.

Red sentiment
Contract issues
Prior implementation scars
High-risk payroll concerns
Fragile custom time-off workflow

We built a custom validation process. We offered them dedicated engineering support for their custom Cognos reports. Normally customers handle their own. We made the exception because the risk profile warranted it. They got through the upgrade after my last day at UKG.

“Clear communication across teams, ensuring all stakeholders remained informed and aligned. A key contributor to the project’s success.”

THERESA MORGAN · SENIOR BUSINESS PARTNER, UKG · APRIL 30 2026

The outcomes

What I saw through April 15.

40% of 300 customers upgraded
↑ velocity rising, not falling
Ahead of the November 2026 delivery date

What the dashboard did not do: change Lisa’s prioritization calls. Her judgment was already sharp. The dashboard’s value was the shared vocabulary it created across functions, not the executive judgment it replaced.

The throughline

Operating mechanisms change what a function can see.

Not what it does. Build the conditions where the right thing becomes the easier thing. The people you’re working alongside will do the rest.
