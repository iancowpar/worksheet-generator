# Mark candidates

Three options for the worksheet generator's primary mark. Each follows
Cold Open's structural rule: one glacier-teal stroke (`#7CC0B8`, 10pt
width, round caps) plus one charcoal dot (`#0B1220`, r=4.5) on a 64×64
viewBox. Pick one, or ask for a fourth direction.

## A — Rising Arc

A 90° arc sweeping from bottom-left to top-right. Dot at the upper end.

**Reads as:** trajectory, growth, lift. The student's path from failed
test to mastery. Closest aesthetic match to Cold Open's forward C —
same gestural single-stroke language, different orientation.

**Best when:** you want a mark that's abstract and aspirational and
sits comfortably in the same brand universe as Cold Open.

## B — Checkmark

A clean check formed from two strokes, joined at the corner. Dot at
the tip of the upstroke.

**Reads as:** got it, correct, success. Most universally legible of
the three — a teacher would understand what the product does at a
glance.

**Best when:** clarity matters more than abstraction, or when the
product needs to feel approachable and concrete to non-design users.
Risk: checkmark logos are common, so distinctiveness rests on the
proportion and the dot.

## C — Mirrored C

A C-arc opening to the left instead of the right (mirror of Cold
Open's mark). Dot inside the opening on the left.

**Reads as:** sibling, complement, "the next chapter." Explicit visual
pair with Cold Open. If the worksheet generator is part of a Cold Open
universe, this signals it instantly.

**Best when:** you want the two products to be visually linked —
pulled out together, they read as a set. Risk: too literal a mirror;
could feel derivative on its own.
