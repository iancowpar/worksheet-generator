The line that pulled me into this posting was "shifting from traditional infrastructure to an AI-augmented ecosystem where speed and reliability are driven by intelligent automation." Most product orgs are still treating that shift as a roadmap item — something to ship to customers. The version that compounds is the one where the same shift happens internally first: the PMs, the engineers, and the stakeholders running on top of the platform are the first beneficiaries of the intelligent automation, not the second. That distinction is the part of this role that I think actually matters.

That's the work I've been doing at UKG. I built a PM automation library and then scaled it into a team-wide shared capability — the first organized AI adoption effort inside the product org. I designed a five-panel defect triage system that classifies open issues across customer escalations, code defects, internal bugs, and CVEs across four products, auto-refreshing daily with a single highest-leverage action surfaced per view. I built sprint health instrumentation refreshing every 15 minutes across two engineering teams, with documented rejection criteria for burndown, velocity, and story-point anti-patterns and technical rationale for each. The pattern is consistent: find the manual feedback synthesis, the documentation tax, the status-report routine — and replace it with something that runs without me. That's exactly the shift this role is asking the platform org to make at scale.
