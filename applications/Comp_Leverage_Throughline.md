**To Deltek (Lisa Martin) when comp comes up:**

> *"I should be transparent that I'm advancing in another late-stage conversation at a Lead PM role with a published band that goes higher than Deltek's. I'm not playing one against the other — I want to make a decision based on the work, the team, and the comp parity math working out. **Why this works:**
- It's true. Both companies have a right to know you have other options. Most senior candidates do.
- It reframes leverage as honesty, not pressure.
- It anchors your comp ask in parity math, not in "what the other guy is offering."
- It gives them the chance to compete on the work and the team, not just on cash.

## Decision tree if both offers come in

**Scenario A: Both offers within range, similar timing.**
Choose based on the work and the team. Comp likely converges within $10-20K. Use each offer to nudge the other up at the margins, but don't let comp be the deciding factor between two offers in range.

**Scenario B: Suffolk in range, Deltek below walk-away.**
Take Suffolk, decline Deltek gracefully but explore whether the CoS path is still alive. Don't burn the Deltek relationship — they're still a company you'd consider in the future.

**Scenario C: Deltek in range, Suffolk below walk-away.**
Take Deltek. Suffolk's $180K floor is reasonable for the role; below that, the commute math doesn't work.

**Scenario D: Both below walk-away.**
Pause. Both companies are still in the pipeline; the right move may be to push CoS at Deltek harder or look at the other pipeline opportunities (Aha!, Hyland, Goodpath, Gusto, Jerry.ai).

**Scenario E: One offer arrives weeks before the other.**
Negotiate a brief extension on the early offer. *"I'm wrapping up another process and would like a week to make a thoughtful decision."* Both companies will respect this from a senior candidate.

## What NOT to do

- **Don't lie about offer status.** "I have another offer" when you don't is the fastest way to burn a relationship.
- **Don't disclose the specific company.** "I'm in another late-stage conversation" is fine; "Suffolk is offering me X" is not. Specifics give them ammunition to call your bluff if you're bluffing.
- **Don't make comp the entire conversation.** You're optimizing for work, manager, and trajectory at least as much as for cash. Show that in how you talk.
- **Don't accelerate timelines artificially.** If Deltek comes in fast and Suffolk is still weeks away, telling Deltek "I need an answer this week" when you don't is theatrical leverage that backfires.
- **Don't burn either bridge.** Both companies are in your network for the long haul. Even if you decline, do it warmly. People move; teams shift; opportunities recur.
