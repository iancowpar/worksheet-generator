# Deltek 30/60/90 Plan — Senior Manager, AI Adoption & Enablement

## Context

Ian is accepting an offer for Senior Manager, AI Adoption & Enablement at Deltek. The role is a player-coach seat (70% IC / 30% management), reports to Lisa Rabideau (VP UX), and owns the design of how a 1,500+ person Product & Technology org actually adopts AI, not just installs it. It's a builder role at the front edge of Deltek's AI transformation, with direct reports including an India-based AI Community Manager and partnership with L&D leads in Makati and Singapore.

This plan covers the period from today through approximately October 1 (Day 90 of employment). It is built around three operating principles Ian has already established with Lisa Rabideau during the HM lunch and the panel rounds:

1. **The builder seat.** Ian is not duplicating the L&D craft (the Makati and Singapore leads hold it). His job is to own the operating system and the measurement model around their work.
2. **Extinction over addition.** Adoption is measured by what stopped, not what launched. The fallback principle and middle-out measurement are the load-bearing frameworks Ian brings.
3. **Decision-ready reporting.** Monthly delivery to CPTO leadership, focused on outcomes rather than activity.

The plan is also shaped by what we already know about the organizational context: annual January RIFs are real (~9% reduction in Jan 2026), so value must be visible early; the role is at Senior Manager but Ian's been positioned by Lynette to grow toward Director within 12-18 months; Deltek's culture has shifted under new leadership toward cost-discipline, so the work needs to demonstrate measurable impact quickly.

**Working assumptions:**
- Start date: approximately Monday July 6, 2026 (the later of the two options offered)
- Day 30: approximately August 3, 2026
- Day 60: approximately September 2, 2026
- Day 90: approximately October 2, 2026
- This is Ian's personal playbook, not a doc to share with Lisa Rabideau wholesale, though specific elements (the measurement model, the first 30-day audit framing) become Lisa-facing artifacts later

## Phase 1: Days 1-30 — Listen and Audit (July 6 → August 3)

Goal: Show up as a listener with rigor, not a strategist with a plan. By Day 30, Ian has a complete picture of the current state of AI adoption inside Deltek's P&T org, the relationships he needs, the systems he'll be operating, and 2-3 hypotheses about where the highest-leverage early work lives.

### Week 1 (July 6-10): Orientation and warm welcome

- Complete Deltek's structured onboarding (HR orientation, IT setup, buddy match)
- First 1:1 with Lisa Rabideau. Use the lunch framing as the through-line: *"I'm here to build the operating system and measurement model around the L&D craft. The first 30 days are listen mode."*
- Meet the existing AI Community Manager in India (the direct report). First call is just human; no agenda. Establish trust before exchanging artifacts.
- Send introductory notes to the L&D leads in Makati and Singapore. Schedule 1:1s for Week 2 to respect their time-zone load.
- Be in the Boston office at least 2-3 days of Week 1. Face-time signal matters in the first week even at a hybrid company.

### Week 2 (July 13-17): First wave of 1:1s

Target meetings with each of:
- The two L&D specialists (Makati and Singapore). 60 minutes each. Goal: understand what they own, what they're working on now, what they wish someone would build around their work.
- The AI Platform team lead. 45 minutes. Goal: understand what tools are deployed, what's in pilot, what the platform team's roadmap is.
- Two or three vertical AI Leads (whichever functions are most active). Goal: understand which use cases they're chasing, what's working, what's stalled.
- The other Deltek panelists Ian already met (Bret Tushaus, Michelle Cipollone, Gourav Kapoor). Reconnect now that Ian is inside. Each one becomes a recurring touchpoint.

Each meeting feeds the 1:1 cadence template. Ian is building a relationship map and a friction map simultaneously.

### Week 3 (July 20-24): System audit

Goal: understand what's currently being measured, by whom, and how.

- **Audit existing AI adoption measurement.** What metrics are leadership seeing today? Where do they come from? Are they Tier 1-2 (usage) or higher? Compare against the 6-tier framework in `AI_Adoption_Measurement_Framework.md`.
- **Audit the champion network.** Does one exist? Who's in it? Is it active or in name only? Are champions producing peer-to-peer adoption, or are they wearing the title without driving outcomes?
- **Audit the existing P&T onboarding for new hires.** What's the current 30/60/90? What measurement, if any, is attached? Where is the role-specific learning gap (the area where employee reviews said training "didn't teach the product")?
- **Audit the recognition platform usage.** What's recognized today? Is it tenure/effort, or AI-adoption and execution behaviors as the JD specifies?
- **Audit recent all-hands content.** Is it strategy or status recap? What's the current cadence and shape?

### Week 4 (July 27-31): Synthesis and first findings

- Spend the week consolidating Week 1-3 observations into a structured assessment. Not a deck. A working document Lisa Rabideau can read in 15 minutes.
- Identify the 2-3 highest-leverage adoption problems based on what's been observed.
- Identify 2-3 candidates for the first measurable intervention (Days 60-90 work).
- Draft a "current state of AI adoption at Deltek P&T" memo, internally for Ian's own clarity. 1,000-1,500 words. The first synthesis artifact.
- Send Lisa Rabideau a Day 30 read-out: what Ian has learned, what hypotheses are forming, what he wants to do in the next 30 days. Keep it tight — one page maximum.

### What to NOT do in Days 1-30

- Don't propose new programs, frameworks, or measurement systems
- Don't critique existing work in 1:1s
- Don't pitch the extinction-over-addition principle as a manifesto. It will land later as evidence-driven, not as a worldview imposed on Day 1.
- Don't try to fix the role-specific onboarding gap yet. That's a Phase 2-3 deliverable.
- Don't push the team to async-first norms unilaterally. Earn the right to propose this through demonstrated time-zone discipline first.

### Artifacts produced by Day 30

- 1:1 cadence documents for ~15-20 stakeholders
- A current-state audit memo (internal use)
- A "decisions deferred" list with rationale for each
- A Day 30 read-out to Lisa Rabideau
- Updated weekly observation log

## Phase 2: Days 30-60 — Diagnose and Design (August 3 → September 2)

Goal: Convert observations into proposals. By Day 60, Ian has a measurement model proposed to Lisa Rabideau, a clear intervention candidate selected for Day 60-90 work, and the beginnings of a champion network reactivation plan.

### Week 5-6 (August 3-14): The measurement model proposal

This is the central artifact of Days 30-60. Build it as a working document, then walk Lisa through it in a 1:1.

The proposal grounds itself in the 6-tier framework from `AI_Adoption_Measurement_Framework.md`:

- **Tier 4 (Workflow integration metrics)** as the leading indicators Deltek should instrument first. Specifically: % of workflows where AI is standard not optional, process steps eliminated, time-to-meaningful contribution for new hires (which the JD explicitly asks for).
- **Tier 5 (Behavior change and habit formation)** as the diagnostic layer. Specifically: extinction over addition (manual workflows that stopped), voluntary effort (custom prompts/templates created), champion network velocity (peer-to-peer adoption rate).
- **Tier 6 (Outcome metrics)** as lagging indicators, used as the executive narrative for CPTO reporting but not as the program steering signal.

The proposal explicitly recommends: capture a 4-week baseline of the leading indicators NOW (Weeks 1-4 of any new tool rollout), so the program can prove what changed.

### Week 7 (August 17-21): Champion network activation plan

- Map the existing champion network state from the Week 3 audit
- Identify the 5-8 champions Ian wants to reactivate or activate
- Define the activation model: cadence, ownership, what champions own vs. what L&D owns vs. what Ian owns
- Define the feedback loop: how champions surface friction back to the program

### Week 8 (August 24-28): Intervention selection and scoping

Pick ONE intervention to ship by Day 90. The criteria:
- Demonstrable behavior change (extinction signal possible)
- Cross-functional reach (not just one team)
- Can be measured with the leading indicators from the measurement model
- Honest scope — shippable in 30 days, not a multi-quarter program

Candidate examples (Ian will identify the actual best one from Days 1-60 audit):
- A specific manual workflow common across PM/Engineering that AI can collapse, paired with a fallback-sunset plan
- A redesign of the role-specific onboarding for one P&T function (e.g., new engineer onboarding) with measurement attached
- A pilot of the "voluntary effort" tracking (custom prompts/templates contributed back to a shared repo) within one pod

Write a 1-page intervention proposal: what it is, why this one, how it's measured, what success looks like at Day 90, what the fallback-sunset plan is.

### Day 60 read-out to Lisa Rabideau

- Current state of AI adoption at Deltek P&T (refined from Day 30 memo)
- The measurement model proposal
- The champion network activation plan
- The Day 60-90 intervention proposal
- Where Ian needs Lisa's air cover, where he's operating on his own

### What to NOT do in Days 30-60

- Don't ship the intervention yet. Scope and design only.
- Don't try to be the operator on every workflow integration question. Push back to the team owners; the role is the operating system around their work, not the work itself.
- Don't propose a full P&T onboarding redesign. Pick one function, prove the pattern, expand later.
- Don't surface the Director conversation yet. Operating at Director level is how it gets earned; bringing it up explicitly in Month 2 is premature.

### Artifacts produced by Day 60

- A measurement model proposal (Lisa-facing)
- A champion network activation plan (working doc)
- A Day 60-90 intervention proposal (Lisa-facing)
- An updated current-state audit memo
- A Day 60 read-out document

## Phase 3: Days 60-90 — Ship and Report (September 2 → October 2)

Goal: By Day 90, Ian has shipped one concrete intervention end-to-end with measurement attached, delivered the first decision-ready monthly report to CPTO leadership, and established the operating cadence that will run the function for the rest of the year.

### Week 9-10 (September 2-12): Ship the intervention

- Execute the Day 60-90 intervention from the Day 60 proposal
- Capture the leading-indicator measurements at deployment (the baseline, if not already captured during Weeks 7-8)
- Run the deliberate-sunset plan in parallel — remove the fallback for the displaced workflow as the new one rolls out
- Document everything as it ships. The shipping process itself becomes the playbook for the next intervention.

### Week 11 (September 14-18): First decision-ready CPTO report

The JD specifies "monthly, decision-ready adoption reporting for CPTO leadership, focused on outcomes rather than activity." This is the first one.

Format principles (drawn from the AI Adoption Measurement Framework):
- Lead with behavior change signal (Tier 5), not usage (Tier 1)
- Include leading indicators alongside lagging indicators with explicit framing of which is which
- Name the workflows that stopped, the workflows that started, and the workflows still in transition
- Include the fallback-availability ratio for each major workflow in transition
- Honest about what isn't yet measurable
- One page maximum. Decision-ready means short.

### Week 12 (September 21-25): Async working norm change

Implement one async working norm change with the global team. Candidates:
- A documented "what gets a meeting vs. what gets a written decision doc vs. what gets a Loom" policy
- A weekly written async update from each direct report in lieu of one synchronous 1:1 per month
- A meeting-free block during Makati morning hours so the L&D leads have focus time

Pick one. Document it. Get team buy-in. Measure whether meeting volume decreased and whether decision velocity held.

### Week 13 (September 28 - October 2): Day 90 synthesis

- Day 90 read-out to Lisa Rabideau: what's been built, what's been shipped, what's been measured, what's next
- Updated personal observation log: what Ian got right, what he got wrong, what changed in his model of the role
- Reflection on the operating mechanism: is the cadence sustainable? What's working?

### Artifacts produced by Day 90

- One intervention shipped with measurement attached
- The first decision-ready CPTO monthly report delivered
- One async working norm change implemented and measured
- A Day 90 read-out to Lisa Rabideau
- A growing "operating system" of templates, audit frameworks, intervention proposals, and measurement reports
- A documented intervention playbook (the second intervention will be faster because of this)

## Day 90 milestone: what "killing it" looks like

By October 2, Ian has demonstrated:

1. **He listens before he proposes.** The Day 30 and 60 read-outs to Lisa show he absorbed before he prescribed, which is the inverse of the failure mode most external hires fall into.
2. **He builds the operating system, not the L&D craft.** The L&D specialists in Makati and Singapore feel partnered with, not over-shouted.
3. **He measures honestly.** The first CPTO report includes both what's working and what isn't yet measurable, which Kapoor (and Rabideau) will register as the honesty signal.
4. **He ships small and real.** One intervention with measurement attached beats three intervention proposals on a roadmap slide.
5. **He understands extinction.** The deliberate-sunset plan accompanying the intervention is the signal that he's operating at the right level — not adding programs, but changing what stopped.
6. **He's earning the Director path.** Operating at Director level (cross-functional alignment, strategic planning, decision-ready reporting) without title is how the re-level conversation gets earned in months 6-18.

## Risks and watch-outs

- **The Makati/India async load is structurally exposed.** Set boundaries in Week 1 — a hard cap on early-morning or late-evening meetings — or the role consumes the personal life. Glassdoor reviews flagged 12-14 hour days for roles bridging Philippines/India.
- **The role's first political test is the L&D specialists.** Treating them as partners not subordinates is non-negotiable. Anything that reads as "the new American Senior Manager telling us how to do our craft" will permanently damage the operating mechanism.
- **The role exists because of an acknowledged gap.** Don't pretend the gap doesn't exist. Don't pretend Ian can fix it in 90 days. The honesty about scope and timeline is the credibility play.
- **Annual January RIFs are real.** The Day 90 milestone has to land before fiscal-year planning conversations begin. Demonstrated value by October 2 protects the role through Jan 2027 RIF cycle.
- **Don't bring up the Director conversation in Months 1-3.** It gets surfaced in months 6-9 when the operating mechanism is proven. Surfacing it early reads as ambition over substance.
