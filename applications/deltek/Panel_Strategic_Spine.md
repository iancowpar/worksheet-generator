# The Seven Meta-Moves — strategic spine

The plays *underneath* the tactics. Every individual decision on the
case studies, slides, dashboard, and live Q&A should be checked against
these seven. If a choice doesn't ladder up to one of them, it's
ornament. If it ladders up to two or more, it's load-bearing.

Saved here as the north-star so it stays visible across the prep files
and doesn't get diluted in the run-up to Wednesday.

## The connective thread

You are not trying to look like the best candidate. You are trying to
be so obviously *the person already doing the job* that hiring you
feels like a formality.

Every meta-move below is a different angle on that one play.

---

## 1. Make the artifact contain its own critique

**The principle.** Name every weakness before the panel can. A
self-critiquing artifact is nearly impossible to attack — there's no
"gotcha" left to land. The Tuesday Test, applied to your own work.

**Where it's applied.**
- Dashboard: the *Tried and lapsed* card, *What didn't work* card, the
  *Honest version of value* caveat, the methodology note.
- Slides (planned): mid-case study explicit failure beat — "month four
  it stalled, here's what I did."
- Case studies: the two risks you named to Forgan — afraid to kill it,
  no baseline at the start.

**Where else to apply.** The Q&A opener: if asked something hard,
*lead with the weakest part of your own answer* before strengthening
it. Counter-intuitive, but it makes you look like the honest narrator
of your own work.

## 2. Demonstrate the job, don't describe it

**The principle.** Never claim a skill — exhibit it. The medium is the
proof. The dashboard isn't a pitch about *how* you'd track adoption;
it's a working instance of you already doing it.

**Where it's applied.**
- Dashboard exists.
- Slides demonstrate design taste.
- Portfolio page demonstrates building in public.
- Substack essay demonstrates the thinking.
- Tuesday Test demonstrates the diagnostic.

**Where else to apply.** In Q&A, if asked "how would you...?", resist
the temptation to describe. Say *"the closest thing I can show you
without writing on the whiteboard is..."* and reference one of the
artifacts. The artifact is the answer.

## 3. One artifact, four native languages

**The principle.** Rather than four separate pitches, give each
panelist a face they recognize in the same artifact. Each person sees
themselves in it and concludes you understand *their* concern.

**Where it's applied.**
- Dashboard: Kapoor's unit economics, Cipollone's narrative hooks,
  Tushaus's behavior-change mechanics, Rabideau's adoption-as-UX.
- Case studies (planned): same logic — every beat lands differently
  for each panelist while you tell one story.

**Where else to apply.** When choosing examples in Q&A, default to
ones that land for two panelists at once. *"The migration-tool
prototype"* lands for Tushaus (shipped) and Kapoor (built with the
platform architect). *"Twenty PMs without a mandate"* lands for
Cipollone (story) and Rabideau (culture as architecture).

## 4. Honesty as the weapon, not the liability

**The principle.** In a room of AI-fatigued executives, the candidate
who *under-claims* and shows the mess is the differentiated one.
Honesty isn't de-risking — it's winning.

**Where it's applied.**
- "Hours recovered, not banked cash" reframe in the dashboard.
- $4.14 honest cost-per-hour instead of $2.76 seat-only.
- 11 tried-and-lapsed, named.
- "I haven't worked digital preservation" / "I haven't owned SRE
  rotations" in the cold outreach.
- Forgan: the two risks of the program you named before being asked.

**Where else to apply.** Anywhere the prep is tempted to round up.
Don't. The conservative number is the credible one.

## 5. Make the leveling argument through the work, never out loud

**The principle.** Never say "I operate above this title." Build
something only someone at that level could produce, and let Karen
Alden reach the conclusion herself when she reads the debrief. The
artifact does the arguing; your mouth doesn't have to.

**Where it's applied.**
- Dashboard is the leveling argument made tangible — a Sr Manager
  candidate doesn't normally arrive with a working operating
  mechanism.
- The two case studies' breadth (behavior change + planning/risk).
- The post-Forgan email's *"clearer now about the level the work
  itself implies"* — the most you should ever say out loud.

**Where else to apply.** In the panel, when comp/level questions
arise, deflect to the work. *"I'd want to see what the work looks
like in practice before anchoring on a title."* Lets Karen socialize
upward.

## 6. Pre-empt, don't react

**The principle.** War-game the room's objections *before* the room.
Convert every potential ambush into a prepared, calm answer. The
person who's already thought of the hard question looks like the
person who's done the job.

**Where it's applied.**
- Panel Council convened against the dashboard, with verbal answers
  for live Q&A.
- Anticipated questions doc for Forgan (worked).
- Driscoll lens for Forgan (worked).
- The four panelist dossiers + StoryBrand stories + meta-moves.

**Where else to apply.** Before any unscheduled high-stakes
conversation in the next week (Mark Monday, Tasos decision, possible
HM rounds), run the same drill — three to five questions you most
hope you don't get, with prepared answers.

## 7. Align on belief, not phrasing

**The principle.** Operate in each panelist's belief layer without
parroting their words back. Recognition without mirroring. Quoting
"systems of reason" makes you look like you read the press release;
*believing* what they believe and saying it your way makes you a peer.

**Where it's applied.**
- Tushaus's "culture not tooling" — your case studies are built on
  the same conviction in your own language.
- Kapoor's FinOps rigor — your dashboard governs adoption spend the
  same way without using "FinOps" anywhere except where natural.
- Hughes (UKG-flavored CEO) — your stories are natively legible
  without anyone naming UKG.

**Where else to apply.** Whenever the temptation arises to use
someone's own phrase back at them. Don't. Trust the belief layer.

---

## How to use this doc

Before any other prep file gets edited or any conversation gets
drafted, the question to ask: *which of the seven does this serve?*
If the answer isn't clear, the move is probably ornament and the
energy should go elsewhere. If two or more apply, it's load-bearing
and worth perfecting.

The seven do not need to be named in the room. They are the operating
system you run on, not the message you deliver.
