# Forgan — Anticipated Questions

Drill material for Tuesday May 26, 3pm ET. Built on the Driscoll lens,
the Brooks lens, Lynette's read (*"introspective / situational"*), and
the substance in `PreCall_Card_Jamie_Forgan.md`.

## The shape of the hour

Forgan is unlikely to run a checklist. Quiet, long-tenured operators
who ask introspective questions tend to run *conversational*
interviews — fewer questions, more time per answer, lots of *tell me
about a time*. He's evaluating "can I imagine working with this
person across the seam between product and engineering" more than
"does this resume check the boxes." That's good news. The Brooks lens
applies cleanly here: conversation as coordination, not performance.

The questions below are not in interview order — they're grouped by
function. Forgan will likely ask 8-12 of them, not 30. Prep the
shapes; don't memorize answers.

---

## 1 — The opener

### "Tell me about yourself and what drew you to this role."

*What it's testing:* whether you frame a clean through-line without
over-pitching. Quiet operators are allergic to rehearsed pitches.

*The angle:* lead on the migration-tool / built-with-engineering
through-line. 60 seconds max. Anchor on something like *"I keep
ending up at the seam between product, engineering, and AI."* When
you mention the role, frame it as the formalization of work you've
already been doing without mandate — not as something you want, but
as something you've already been operating inside.

### "Any questions about Deltek before we get going?"

*What it's testing:* whether you've done the research, and whether
you'll treat this as a peer exchange or a quiz.

*The angle:* one clarifying question to get him talking — the
prepared *"what does your engineering org cover today?"* is the right
opener-question (already in the precall card). Save the
relationship-with-the-role question for the end.

## 2 — Situational, past-work (high probability)

### "Walk me through something you shipped recently you're proud of."

*The angle:* migration tool first. The arc: I prototyped it myself,
partnered with the platform architect to define what was buildable,
handed engineering a complete delivery package, Services is running
it self-serve. The "built-with-not-adjacent-to" through-line. DON'T
lead with the AI capability layer — Forgan will code that as program
work, not engineering work.

### "Tell me about a time engineering pushed back on something product wanted to ship."

*What it's testing:* the Driscoll question in disguise. He wants to
know whether you defer when engineering is right, or push regardless.

*The angle:* a real moment where engineering's pushback changed your
direction and the outcome was better for it. End on *engineering was
right, the change made the work better, I learned something.* No
"and then they realized I was right" energy.

### "How do you handle the tension between product roadmap and engineering's read of what's load-bearing?"

*The angle:* it's a signal, not a problem. Engineering knows things
about the system product doesn't. The job isn't to win the argument
— it's to figure out which side has the better-shaped information
for the call. Sometimes that's engineering, sometimes that's product,
often a third option surfaces when both sides actually talk to each
other. This answer is *anti-Driscoll-posture* without being
self-righteous about it.

### "Tell me about something that didn't work."

*What it's testing:* whether you've actually run AI rollouts or just
have a tidy origin story. Driscoll-types respect failure stories more
than success stories.

*The angle:* pick a real one. The AI capability layer didn't start at
twenty PMs — it started at three, and not all three stuck. Or: a
specific Claude Code slash command you built that nobody used and you
eventually retired. Specific. Flat. No performance of humility.

## 3 — The hard Driscoll test (already prepped)

### "What if my engineers don't want what you're bringing?"

Answer is in the precall card. The line:

> "Then I haven't brought them the right thing yet. The work has to
> make an engineer's week better, not give them another stakeholder
> to satisfy. I'd rather scope it down or kill it than push something
> that doesn't actually serve the person who has to live with it."

---

## 4 — AI-specific

### "How do you use AI in your own work day-to-day?"

*What it's testing:* fluency, not strategy. Are you actually
operating with AI or theorizing about it.

*The angle:* specific workflows. The twenty slash commands. Claude
Code as your prototyping environment, not a chat interface. Be ready
to name two or three things you do daily that you couldn't have done
two years ago. Concrete > abstract every time.

### "How do you measure AI adoption?"

*The angle:* extinction over addition. What stopped, not what
launched. Behavior change in named workflows, not seats activated.
The Tuesday Test diagnostic (the Substack essay) can come up
naturally if he asks; don't pitch it.

### "What's actually working in enterprise AI right now, and what's theater?"

*The angle:* working — AI inside bounded workflows with a clear
owner who measures behavior. Theater — anything called "AI
transformation" without a specific workflow attached. The Starbucks
retirement is a clean current example if it lands.

## 5 — Introspective (Lynette's flag)

### "Why are you looking?"

*What it's testing:* real reason vs. job-shopping. And whether you'll
talk down a previous employer (Forgan codes that as a red flag —
he's been at the same place 18 years).

*The angle:* brief, honest, forward-looking. The honest read: you've
built the AI capability layer at UKG as a side function with no
mandate, and you want to do that work with formal scope and a function
around it. Deltek is the place where this role exists by design.
Close on what's drawing you to Deltek, not what's pushing you out of
UKG.

### "What part of your work do you find draining?"

*The angle:* real answer. *"Performative work"* or *"meetings where
nothing actually gets decided."* Don't say "nothing" — he'll know
that's not true.

### "What kind of manager or culture do you work best inside?"

*The angle:* honest specifics that double as a quiet read of Forgan's
own preference. Decisions made close to the work. Engineering treated
as a peer, not a resource. Adoption measured in behavior, not seats.
The description fits his world — he'll hear that.

### "Where do you want this role to take you in three years?"

*The angle:* Principal-scope leader in AI adoption, ideally with a
function under you, ideally still inside Deltek if the work and the
people are right. Don't pretend you'll be there forever; don't
signal you'll leave in three either.

## 6 — Planning / scope

### "What would your first 30 / 60 / 90 days look like?"

*The angle:*
- **30:** listen tour. Forgan's team, Rabideau's team, Cipollone's
  team, Tushaus's team, Cloud Architecture. Find workflows that quietly
  retired themselves already — those people are the early champion
  network.
- **60:** pick three specific personas / workflows to instrument.
- **90:** ship the first measurable behavior-change report. Don't
  promise a launch; promise a measurement.

### "Where does this role conflict with engineering ownership?"

*The angle:* there's a clean line. The role doesn't own the
engineering roadmap. The role surfaces the signal that helps
engineering deprioritize, sustain adoption, and avoid building AI
capabilities people won't use. Engineering owns what gets built; the
role owns whether what gets built actually changes how people work.

---

## 7 — Closing

### "Anything you wanted to make sure we covered?"

Have one substantive thing ready. The honest one — *"the working
relationship between your org and this role. What does the partnership
look like in practice, what does it look like when it goes well, what
does it look like when it goes badly."*

That question is the Driscoll-lens turned outward. It signals you
take the partnership seriously and you want the truth about it, not
the brochure.

---

## What NOT to do (Driscoll watch-outs)

- **Don't lead with the AI capability layer.** Reads as program work.
- **Don't pitch MCP / Anthropic / where-AI-is-going.** Save for panel.
- **Don't perform the boomerang-from-RIF story unprompted.** Flat if
  asked, brief, not narrated.
- **Don't talk over silences.** Forgan will use them. So can you.
- **Don't name Deltek anywhere else when discussing live processes.**
  "Several live processes."
- **Don't claim to know his scope.** Ask early, lean into his answer.
