## Important · do if time

### 5. The opener — first 30 seconds settling

You don't perform the start; you settle into it. *"Thanks for the
hour. Before I get into the cases, quick frame…"* and then the
Starbucks cold open. The discipline: no apology, no throat-clearing,
no "I'm so excited to be here." Just present in the room. Worth
practicing the exact first 30 seconds once aloud.

### 6. Transition language between sections

The five transitions: cold open → Case 1 → bridge → Case 2 → close
→ Q&A. Each is one or two sentences. Worth pre-writing so you don't
stumble into a "so, um, next…" moment.

### 7. Disarms for ambush questions

If you get a question you genuinely don't have an answer for, the
disarm is honest curiosity, not stalling. Pre-loaded:
- *"I'd want to think about that more carefully — what's the read
  you're working with?"*
- *"That's not something I've solved. The closest analog I've
  worked on is X."*
- *"Honestly, I'd want to be in the role for 30 days before I'd
  trust my answer to that."*

The pattern: brief acknowledgment + pivot to what you do know +
genuine question back if relevant.

### 8. Post-panel follow-up emails — one per panelist

Same pattern as the Forgan note. Brief, references a specific
substantive moment from the conversation, doesn't summarize what was
discussed. Drafted ahead, finalized in the 60 minutes after the
panel ends. Four short emails, ~10 minutes each.
