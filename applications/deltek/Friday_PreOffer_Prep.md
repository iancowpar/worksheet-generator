# Deltek final-phase prep — 9am Lisa Martin + Friday Lisa Rabideau

Two events. The 9am call sets up Friday. Friday is the offer gate.

---

## Event 1 — 9am call with Lisa Martin (recruiter)

### Purpose

Lisa briefs you on Friday. You confirm logistics, raise the parallel-track
situation per Lynette's guidance, and ask the diagnostic questions that
help you prep for Rabideau.

This is mostly a listening call. Your job is to be calm, ask sharp
questions, and surface the parallel-process info as professional
courtesy.

### The parallel-track moment — deliver early in the call

Don't bury this. Get it out as your second or third substantive thing
after the pleasantries. Lynette's framing locked the register:

> "Before we go too far, one thing I want to be transparent about. I'm
> in late stages with two other processes that may move on similar
> timing. I hadn't brought it up earlier because I didn't have
> certainty on any of them, but now that we're at pre-offer stage with
> Deltek, I want to make sure you and Lisa Rabideau have visibility,
> both as a courtesy given the time you're investing on your side and
> so we can plan timing accordingly."

What this does:
- Frames as courtesy, not negotiation move (Lynette's word)
- Pre-empts any "why didn't you say earlier" friction
- Doesn't name names or numbers
- Signals timing pressure naturally without manufacturing it

### Questions Lisa will likely ask in response

**"What's the timeline on the others?"**
> "Both could move this week or next. I'm trying to align timing so I
> can make a complete decision rather than a sequential one."

**"Are they similar roles?"**
> "Both AI-adjacent. Different shapes."

No more specifics. Don't volunteer companies, comp, or anything else.

**"Where are they in the comp range?"**
> "I'd rather not get into specifics on other processes. Happy to talk
> about what would work for me here when we're at offer."

**"Is there a hard deadline?"**
> "I'm trying to make a complete decision in the next 7-10 days. If one
> of the others moves faster, I'll let you know."

**"How are you feeling about Deltek versus the others?"**
> "Genuinely positive. The conversations here have been substantive,
> and the role itself is the most interesting of the three on
> substance. That's why I'm being transparent with you now."

### Questions you ASK Lisa (pick 2-3, depending on flow)

1. **"What's the format and purpose of Friday? What does Lisa Rabideau
   want to come out of it?"** — this is the most useful question, it
   tells you what register to walk in with.
2. **"Anything specific the panel raised that I should be prepared to
   address in person?"** — gold question. Lisa probably has feedback
   from the panel debrief and may surface concerns you can preempt.
3. **"What's the typical timeline from this Friday to an actual
   offer?"** — calibrates how fast the Deltek clock runs after Friday.
4. **"Should I expect comp discussion to come up Friday, or is that a
   separate conversation?"** — tells you whether to walk in ready to
   talk numbers.
5. **"Anything you'd recommend I bring or prepare?"** — open-ended,
   lets her offer color.

### Tone

Warm, peer, calm. Lisa is your ally. She's been working hard for you
since the start. Treat the parallel-track raise as a professional
courtesy to her specifically, not as leverage against Deltek.

### What NOT to do

- Don't volunteer comp expectations
- Don't name UKG or Preservica
- Don't suggest you've decided anything
- Don't ask logistics questions she'll email about anyway

## Event 2 — Friday in-person with Lisa Rabideau

### What Rabideau wants out of Friday (the most likely shape)

1. **Chemistry confirmation.** Does she actually want to work with you
   day-to-day. This is the silent agenda of every pre-offer meeting.
2. **Final values check.** Deltek's culture, your working style, the
   way you'd operate inside the org.
3. **Resolution of any concerns the panel surfaced.** Lisa Martin may
   tell you these on the 9am call. If she does, walk in ready to
   address them.
4. **Confidence in the hire.** She needs to leave the meeting ready to
   sign the offer letter mentally. Your job is to make that easy.
5. **30/60/90 thinking.** What would you actually DO in your first
   months. Not pitched as a deck; just be able to talk it through.

### Likely concern areas she'll probe (be ready for these)

Based on the JD and what Lynette has surfaced about Rabideau's
priorities:

**Player-coach experience.** The JD asks for someone managing 2-3
direct reports including an India-based AI Community Manager. Your
direct people-management experience is thin (your honest answer to
Tasos was no formal experience but enjoyed coaching). She'll probe
this.

Frame to land:
> "My direct management experience is light. What I've done a lot of
> is leading peers and cross-functional partners without authority,
> which is the closer muscle to managing distributed contributors than
> sitting above a single team. With three reports, my first move would
> be a structured one-on-one cadence with each, written role
> expectations, and getting clear on which decisions I want them owning
> versus which I want to be in the loop on. The biggest mistake I'd
> guard against is becoming a bottleneck for an India-based contributor
> just because of time zones."

**Asia-Pacific async collaboration.** The JD emphasizes this. You
haven't worked deeply across IST and PHT.

Frame to land:
> "I worked across Hyderabad, Delhi, and Bangalore teams at Broadridge
> for years, so time-zone discipline is muscle memory. What I'd want
> to do early is build the async working norms with the India PM and
> the Makati L&D team: what gets a synchronous meeting, what gets a
> written decision doc, what gets a Loom. The fewest live meetings
> that still maintain trust, that's the operating model."

**L&D depth.** Two L&D specialists in Makati and Singapore already
hold the craft. The JD doesn't ask you to be an instructional
designer.

Frame (this is the one you already landed with her at lunch — bring
it forward):
> "I'm not coming in to out-expert the L&D specialists on instructional
> design. They have that craft. My job is to own the operating system
> and the measurement model around what they do, make their work
> visible to leadership through decision-ready reporting, and tell the
> difference between adoption that's real and adoption that's
> performance."

**The Microsoft 365 stack.** The JD names Teams, SharePoint, Viva,
Power Automate as required. You've used Teams. The others may be a
stretch.

Frame:
> "Teams, SharePoint, and basic Power Automate I've used as an
> operator. Viva I'd ramp on quickly. My pattern on new platforms is
> the same one I used at UKG with the platform architect — sit with
> the system, learn the data model, write requirements that respect
> the constraints. The lift on Viva won't be the bottleneck."

### Your 30/60/90 framing (have this ready)

Don't bring a deck. Be able to talk through it conversationally.

**First 30 days — listen mode.**
- 1:1s with the existing AI Community Manager, L&D leads in Makati and
  Singapore, the AI Platform team leads, vertical AI Leads, and a
  handful of PMs/engineers across the cohort
- Audit the current state of adoption: what's measured, what's not,
  what's working, what's pretending to work
- Read the existing onboarding flow, recognition data, all-hands
  content as data points

**Days 30-60 — diagnose and propose.**
- Surface the 2-3 highest-leverage adoption problems based on what
  you've seen
- Bring leadership a measurement model proposal (leading and lagging
  indicators, defined ahead of any new programme launch)
- Identify the champion-network gaps and propose the activation model

**Days 60-90 — ship something small.**
- One concrete intervention shipped end-to-end with measurement
  attached
- First decision-ready monthly report delivered to CPTO leadership
- One async working norm change with the global team
- The honest principle: small, real, measured. Not big, theoretical,
  unmeasured.

The closing frame:
> "The shape of the work I'd want to do is what we landed on at lunch
> — building the operating system and the measurement model around
> the L&D craft, not duplicating the craft itself."

### Questions YOU ask Rabideau (have 4-5 ready)

These signal you're already thinking like her partner.

1. **"Twelve months in, what does a great hire look like to you in
   this role? What's the version of this that lands?"** — opens her up
   on success criteria.
2. **"What's the current health of the champion network? Active and
   self-sustaining, or in need of activation?"** — diagnostic, signals
   you know that's a real make-or-break.
3. **"How do you imagine the partnership with the Makati L&D leads
   working operationally — who owns what, where are the seams?"** —
   surfaces the ownership question early.
4. **"What's the cadence and shape of decision-ready reporting you
   want to CPTO leadership? Monthly is in the JD — what format have
   you been working with?"** — shows you've read carefully.
5. **"Where do you want me pushing back on you, and where do you want
   me running on my own?"** — borrowed from Helena prep but works
   here too. Shows you understand the role as ownership.

Pick 2-3 in flow, don't drill all 5.

### The comp moment (if it happens)

Lisa Martin will tell you on the 9am call whether comp is expected to
come up Friday. Two paths:

**If comp DOES come up:**
- Don't anchor low. Let her name a range first if she will.
- If she asks what you're looking for: *"I'm targeting the top of the
  band Lisa shared with me. What I really care about is the full
  picture — base, bonus structure, sign-on, equity, and benefits. I'd
  want to see all four to evaluate it properly."*
- Don't accept a verbal offer in the room. *"Thank you. I'd want to
  see this in writing and take 24-48 hours to review."*

**If comp DOES NOT come up:**
- Don't raise it. Stay on substance. Comp comes via Lisa Martin
  separately.

### Logistics to settle in advance

- **Location.** Lisa Martin will introduce you to Rabideau by email so
  you can pick somewhere. Suggest something mid-point between
  Northborough (her home base) and Methuen (yours). Coffee shop or
  quiet hotel lobby. Avoid noisy lunch spot.
- **Time of day.** Morning is usually better for a high-stakes
  in-person — fresher minds, no end-of-day fatigue. Push for
  mid-morning if she's flexible.
- **What to wear.** Business casual. She's a VP at a mature company.
  Sport coat over a button-down, no tie. You're not auditioning;
  you're meeting a future colleague.
- **What to bring.** Not a deck. Maybe a small notebook (real one, not
  iPad). The talk script and case narratives stay at home. You've
  already delivered them.
- **What to read in the morning.** Re-read Lisa's published blog posts
  (in the dossier — voice of the user, work the way you think). Hear
  her vocabulary in your head.

### The mindset

Different from the panel. The panel was performance — case studies,
deck, structured arc. Friday is conversation. Two people who already
know each other's work, figuring out whether they want to work
together.

Drop the Driscoll-as-performer frame. Pick up the Driscoll-as-
collaborator frame. Settled curiosity. Genuine interest in HER. Ask
follow-up questions to her answers (Brooks's research: follow-ups
raise likability more than any other conversational move).

You're not trying to land the offer in this meeting. You're trying to
walk out of it with both of you thinking "yeah, that would work."
