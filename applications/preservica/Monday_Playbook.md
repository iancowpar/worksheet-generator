# Mark / Preservica — Monday call playbook

The full call arc, in order. Companion to `PreCall_Card_Recruiter_Monday.md`
and `Strategic_Thesis.md`. Use this as the talk-through; use those
for the deeper context.

## Time budget

30 minutes typical. Rough split:
- Mark opens, sets agenda (~2 min)
- Your "about me" (~2-3 min)
- His questions to you (~12-15 min)
- Your questions to him (~7-10 min)
- Logistics / next steps wrap (~2 min)

Don't try to drive the call. Mark drives. Your job is calibrated
honesty + curiosity + showing the operator profile.

---

## 1 · The opening — your "about me" (60–90 seconds)

When Mark asks *"can you walk me through your background"* or *"tell
me about yourself"* — this is the script. Roughly 140 words, ~55
seconds spoken. Don't memorize it. Internalize the shape.

> "Quick frame. A decade at Broadridge in compliance-grade financial
> services, where wrong outputs had regulatory consequence rather
> than just bug tickets. Then two years at UKG as a Senior PM, where
> the most distinctive work was building the organization's first AI
> capability layer from zero — no top-down mandate, scaled across
> twenty PMs. I also shipped a Claude Code function embedded as a
> routing layer inside an analytics product, and I write weekly on
> AI adoption realism. The gap between deployed and actually adopted
> is the thread I keep coming back to.
>
> I'm in market. UKG had a RIF in April, so I'm actively
> interviewing. The roles I'm focused on sit at the crossing of
> regulated-industry context plus shipped AI, and Preservica showed
> up that way. Government and archive customers don't tolerate AI
> theater, and the JD made the operating-discipline expectation clear."

**What this opener does in 90 seconds:**
- Names the regulated-industry decade upfront — the load-bearing fit signal
- Names UKG and the AI capability layer — credibility on shipped AI
- Surfaces the Substack thought leadership without naming it as a brand
- Honest about the RIF without making it the headline
- Names *why Preservica* without flattering them
- Ends on the JD line they wrote themselves — quiet alignment

**Don't add:**
- Apologetic framing for the RIF
- "I'm so excited about Preservica"
- The full Claude Code productivity OS detail (save for an AI question)
- Anything about the Tuesday Test essay (save for an AI question)

## 2 · Mark's likely questions, in roughly the order he'll ask them

### Q1 — "What drew you to this role specifically?"

> "Three things specifically. First, 'AI Programmes' as a title —
> programmatic framing, not feature PM. That's where I do my best
> work. Second, the customer base. Governments, archives, libraries
> are inherently risk-averse and compliance-driven, which is the
> environment I came up in at Broadridge. AI adoption inside that
> audience has a different bar than at a startup, and I've been
> writing about exactly that. Third, the explicit honesty in the JD
> — 'production-grade capabilities, not experimentation for its own
> sake.' That's the operating discipline I'd want to bring."

Three specific, none generic. Don't add a fourth.

### Q2 — "Walk me through your AI experience"

> "Two years of shipped AI, not just experimentation. The capability
> layer at UKG was the largest piece — built from zero across twenty
> PMs without top-down mandate, measured by what manual workflows
> people retired rather than what tools launched. I embedded a Claude
> Code function as the routing layer inside an analytics product
> that cut a defect list around 97%. I also run a personal Claude
> Code productivity OS — twenty custom slash commands in active
> daily use. And I write weekly on AI adoption realism. Most recent
> piece was on the Starbucks inventory tool retirement, the gap
> between knowing and acting."

This answer gives Mark three concrete artifacts (capability layer,
embedded function, productivity OS) plus the Substack reference.
Enough specificity to be credible; not so much that you lecture.

### Q3 — "Tell me about a challenging cross-functional project"

The migration tool story is the cleanest one to lead with:

> "The cleanest example is a zero-to-one product I shipped concept-to-
> production in a one-week prototype cycle. I wrote the prototype
> myself in Claude Code, scoped three business epics with the
> platform architect to define what was actually buildable, then
> handed engineering a complete delivery package — epics, stories,
> sequencing, the roadmap. Services has been running it self-serve
> since. Projected ~30% time reduction in a ticket-gated enterprise
> workflow. The cross-functional piece was the platform-architect
> partnership — built with engineering as a peer, not at them."

The phrase *"built with engineering as a peer, not at them"* lands
across registers. Use it.

### Q4 — "How would you approach building AI Programmes at Preservica?"

This is the question where most candidates over-claim. Don't.

> "First thirty days I'd be in listen mode — sitting with the team
> you've got, understanding what's actually shipping, where the
> customer asks are coming from, and where Microsoft Copilot
> integration is creating expectations the product needs to meet.
> From the JD and what I know about the company, I'd expect to find
> visible bets in flight plus more capacity asks than throughput.
> The work I'd want to do early is define what measurable success
> looks like — adoption signals, not feature launches — and pick a
> small number of initiatives we can defend in twelve months. But I
> don't have enough context yet to commit to specifics, and I
> wouldn't want to pretend I do."

That answer signals operating discipline AND humility about not
knowing the current state. Both register.

### Q5 — "Why did you leave UKG?"

> "RIF in April, workforce reduction. Not performance-related, but
> the function I was in got resized. I've been actively interviewing
> since, and I'm in late stages with several other companies. I'm
> being selective about where I land — looking for the right fit on
> substance more than speed."

Don't elaborate further. Don't pre-empt their next question.

### Q6 — "What's your timeline?"

> "Actively interviewing, in late stages with two other companies.
> If Preservica moves, I'd be ready to advance the process. I'm not
> auctioning, but I'm also not in indefinite-runway mode. The right
> opportunity at a reasonable pace is what I'm looking for."

The phrase *"not auctioning, but not indefinite runway"* is the move.
Honest about urgency without sounding desperate.

### Q7 — "Can you talk a little more about your Salesforce / Microsoft / Preserve365 experience?"

Honest if it surfaces:

> "I haven't worked Preserve365 directly. Microsoft 365 as an
> operator, yes — Microsoft Copilot I've followed closely as part of
> the broader AI ecosystem but haven't shipped against it. The way
> I've come up to speed on every new platform — through the
> platform-architect partnership at UKG and a decade of compliance
> environments at Broadridge — is by sitting with the architect,
> learning the data model and the constraints, and writing
> requirements that respect both."

Doesn't fake expertise you don't have. Signals the platform-learning
pattern.

### Q8 — "What's your comp expectation?"

The band's already been shared. If Mark pushes:

> "You shared the $140-160K range, which I appreciated. My anchor is
> at the top of that — $160K — and what I really care about is the
> full picture: equity structure, bonus, sign-on, and the benefits
> stack. I'd want to understand all four before locking, but I
> wouldn't anchor below $160K for a role this strategically central."

The phrase *"strategically central"* references the role's actual
importance without flattering them. Use it deliberately.

### Q9 — "Are you remote-friendly? Comfortable with UK time zone?"

> "Methuen, Massachusetts. Eastern Time. Fully remote setup, used to
> it. Morning ET overlap with Newbury is comfortable; I've worked
> with distributed teams across Hyderabad, Delhi, and Bangalore for
> years at Broadridge, so time-zone discipline is muscle memory."

The distributed-teams reference quietly signals he won't be a
time-zone whiner.

## 3 · Your questions for Mark, in priority order

You'll get to ask 3-5 questions, maybe more if Mark's chatty. **Lead
with the diagnostic ones; save the comp-detail ones for the back
half.**

### Tier 1 — diagnostic (must ask, in this order)

**Q1: "When you imagine this role going really well twelve months in,
what's the shape of the team's output — visible AI features shipping
that customers point to, or is it more roadmap and program work?"**

Confirms or refutes the strategic thesis. If he answers in shipping
terms, the role is real. If he answers vaguely or in
research/roadmap terms, recalibrate.

**Q2: "Where does this role sit in the company's AI strategy? Is
there an executive sponsor at the leadership level driving the AI
initiative?"**

Tells you if the role has top-cover. A specific name (CPO Stuart,
CEO Mike Quinn, etc.) is the signal. Vague answer = warning.

**Q3: "What's Preservica's stage right now — where are you on the
growth and funding arc?"**

Polite way to ask about exit timing. Mark may not be at liberty to
say, but his tone tells you whether liquidity is on the near horizon.

### Tier 2 — cash math (ask if time)

**Q4: "On comp beyond base — what's the equity structure like? Are
we talking options, phantom shares, something else? And what's
typical at this level?"**

Surfaces the lottery ticket. Mark may need to come back to you on
specifics; that's fine.

**Q5: "What does the bonus structure look like at this level —
target percentage, individual vs. company performance gates?"**

The bonus is meaningful at 15-20% of $150K base — worth knowing.

**Q6: "And the US benefits stack — 401(k) match, healthcare coverage
percentage, PTO, equipment setup?"**

Easy-win territory. PEO companies often under-spec 401(k); knowing
the number tells you what to negotiate later.

### Tier 3 — process + relationship (always end on these)

**Q7: "If this conversation goes well, what does the next stage look
like — who would I meet, expected timeline?"**

Concrete process question. Tells you the urgency from Preservica's
side.

**Q8: "Anything you wanted to make sure we covered, or anything you
think I should know going into the next stage?"**

The relationship-closer. Opens the door for Mark to share what he
sees. Lifers and senior recruiters love this question.

## 4 · Real-time read on Mark's answers

Use these as your live diagnostic on whether to lean in.

**Strong-thesis signals (this role is real, lean in):**
- Names specific AI features in flight, with timelines
- Names an executive sponsor by name
- Acknowledges Microsoft Copilot pressure honestly
- Shares equity structure willingly even if not all specifics

**Weak-thesis signals (recalibrate, maybe deprioritize):**
- Vague about what's shipping
- Can't name a sponsor
- Frames the role as exploratory
- Avoids comp-detail questions

---

## 5 · The discipline reminders (read once before the call)

- **Don't aggress on comp.** Band's known. Surface the diagnostic
  questions. Comp negotiation belongs at offer stage.
- **Don't volunteer "I'm in other processes at higher bands."** Mark
  already knows you're actively interviewing.
- **Don't crib the JD language back at him.** "Production-grade
  capabilities, not experimentation for its own sake" is in your
  opener but used as a quiet alignment signal, not as flattery.
- **Don't perform energy.** Settled curiosity reads stronger than
  enthusiasm.
- **Don't fill silences.** If Mark pauses, let him. Recruiters notice
  candidates who don't rush.
- **Do follow up on his answers.** When he describes the role or
  the team, ask one follow-up *in his frame* before introducing
  yours. Brooks's research: follow-up questions raise likability
  more than any other conversational move.

---

## 6 · The close

Mark will likely wrap with "any final questions" or "we'll be in
touch." Your close:

> "This has been useful — thank you for the directness on band and
> for the read on the role. I'm interested. Whatever the next step
> is, I'll be ready."

Direct, warm, doesn't oversell. *"I'll be ready"* is the small
confidence move that's better than "looking forward to hearing from
you."

---

## After

Same-day brief thank-you email to Mark. Short. Reference one
specific thing from the conversation. Don't re-pitch. The prep card
has the template framing.
