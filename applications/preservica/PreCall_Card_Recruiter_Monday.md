# Preservica — Senior PM, AI Programmes · Recruiter screen prep

Monday morning recruiter screen. The role is **Senior Product Manager,
AI Programmes** at Preservica. Hybrid UK or Remote US. Reports to Head
of Product.

## What Preservica does (and why it matters for fit)

Active Digital Preservation. Long-term safeguarding of critical digital
information for governments, archives, libraries, and enterprises. The
customer base is inherently risk-averse and compliance-driven — these
aren't "move fast and break things" buyers. They're the same audience
shape that financial services and other regulated industries serve.

The role is about **embedding AI responsibly** into a platform whose
customers will be allergic to AI theater. *"Production grade
capabilities, not experimentation for its own sake"* is in the JD.
That's not boilerplate — it's a worldview statement about who they
want to hire.

## Anticipated recruiter questions

### "Tell me about yourself / why this role?"

60 seconds. Lead on regulated-industries plus AI builder. Anchor
language like: *"I keep ending up at the intersection of regulated
industries, AI, and product. A decade at Broadridge in financial
services where wrong outputs had regulatory consequence. Two years at
UKG building an AI capability layer from zero across twenty PMs. The
role at Preservica reads like the formalization of work I've already
been doing — applying AI responsibly inside a platform whose customers
take long-term consequence seriously."*

### "Why are you looking to leave UKG?"

Brief, forward-looking, no UKG-bashing. The honest version: you've
been building the AI capability layer as a side function without
formal scope or mandate. The Preservica role exists by design — Senior
PM with AI programmes ownership reporting to Head of Product. That's
what you want next.

### "What's your AI experience specifically?"

Don't recite a tools list. Tell two specific stories:
- The migration tool prototype in Claude Code → engineering delivery
  package → projected ~30% time reduction. Shows shipped, not demoed.
- The five-panel analytics product with the embedded Claude Code
  function as routing layer, cutting a defect list ~97%. Shows AI
  inside a product, not adjacent to it.

Then if there's room, the AI capability layer story (twenty PMs, no
mandate).

### "How do you think about AI responsibility / risk?"

This is the question to be most ready for, given the JD. Your real
answer: *"The pattern I keep seeing in enterprise AI is that
deployment gets called adoption. The honest measurement principle I
work to is 'extinction over addition' — count what workflows stopped,
not what tools launched. Responsibility starts at measurement. If you
can't tell honestly whether the AI is replacing work or just adding
to it, you can't govern it."*

Then reference the Starbucks story — the AI inventory tool retired
nine months in. The workers told them in February; the org acted in
May. The gap between knowing and acting is where responsibility
fails. That's the topic of the Tuesday Test essay you published this
week, if natural to mention.

### "What kind of role are you looking for?"

Senior IC with real ownership. Not management track. Reporting into
product leadership (Head of Product matches). Comp band aligned with
US Senior PM market.

### "What's your timeline?"

Realistic. You're actively in process at one or two other places
(don't name them by name). You'd want to be honest that this isn't a
12-week leisurely search — if Preservica wants to move, you can move.

### "What are your comp expectations?"

The email asking for the band goes out before the call (per the
earlier draft). If they didn't share in advance, ask early on the call.
Pre-loaded answer if pressed first: *"I'd want to understand the band
you have for the role before anchoring myself. My current frame is
the US Senior PM market — $160K+ base, with the package considered as
a whole. Happy to be more specific once I know what you're working
with."*

That answer:
- Doesn't anchor low
- Frames you as serious about market alignment, not desperate
- Invites them to share, which is what you want

### "Can you work the UK hybrid?"

No. Remote US is the only path. Don't apologize. *"I'm US-based
(Massachusetts), so I'd be remote on the US side."* Simple.

## Watch-outs

- **Don't bash UKG.** Forward-looking, no critique.
- **Don't oversell domain expertise you don't have.** You haven't
  worked digital preservation. Don't fake it. The JD doesn't require
  it.
- **Don't sound like a "Senior PM, AI" who needs to position around
  the AI gap.** You don't have an AI gap. You have shipped AI work.
  Talk about it that way.
- **Don't anchor low on comp.** UK-anchored postings sometimes try to
  bring US candidates in below US-market bands. If you've gotten the
  band in advance and it's below $130K base, you can decide whether
  to take the call or not. If it's above, take the call.
- **Don't reference the panel content you've prepped for Deltek.**
  Different process. Different customer. Keep the case studies as
  proof points, not as a deck you're pre-pitching.
- **Don't ask "is this remote-friendly?"** It's in the JD. Asking
  signals you didn't read.

## Closing — the question to ask the recruiter

Have one substantive question ready. The good one for this role:

*"What does success in the first year look like for whoever lands
this role? When the Head of Product debriefs in twelve months, what's
the version of the story where they say 'we got this hire right'?"*

That question:
- Signals you think in outcomes
- Surfaces what they actually care about (which feeds the next round)
- Sets up the comp conversation around scope, not just title
- Is a question the recruiter will repeat to the HM, which puts you
  in front of them at the right altitude
