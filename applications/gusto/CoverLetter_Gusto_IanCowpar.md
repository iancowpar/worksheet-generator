Most product orgs that say they need a PM who can scale down from enterprise to SMB are framing the move backwards. The harder version of the problem isn't SMB — it's the compliance, reliability, and multi-stakeholder constraints of enterprise. The design discipline of building a self-serve flow that absorbs complexity behind the scenes is the same in both directions. The customer just has less patience for complexity in one of them, which is a constraint to embrace rather than a skill to acquire. The migration tool I just shipped at UKG was the harder version. The contractor product space is the easier version — same design discipline, less patience for friction.

Here's what I built. The migration tool was a 0→1 product. It didn't exist. It came to me as a consultant-led process — customers who'd already licensed the platform still needed engineering resources to get there. I owned it from idea through three business-epic decomposition (UI, rules, deployment), built the working prototype myself in Claude Code, designed the UX feedback loop and persona, and handed engineering a complete package: bepics, epics, stories, Gantt chart, roadmap. It's in production now. The Services org runs it without us.

The piece I'd want to underline: I wrote the prototype myself in code. Not a spec, not a Figma flow — a working version. That's the part of *"boundaryless builder, AI as your co-builder"* in your job description that I think most candidates won't have a real receipt for. I treat AI as the thing that lets me ship the working version of a product instead of a description of one. That's what changed about being a PM in 2026, and most PM job descriptions still haven't caught up. Yours has.

The discovery muscle I've sharpened is enterprise — escalation rooms, pre-upgrade calls, compliance-grade financial services deployments at Broadridge for a decade before UKG.
