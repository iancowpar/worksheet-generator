# Tasos HM Interview — prep through Alison Wood Brooks's lens

For a Tasos HM interview on the UKG ESE CX Product Owner role. Not yet
confirmed — Steve carries the message to Tasos on Tuesday (May 26); this is
ready-when-it-lands. Written in Brooks's voice as conversation-science
coaching. Pairs with the channel/strategy detail in
`Dara_Followup_And_Salesforce_Framing.md` and the comp detail in
`UKG_Comp_Strategy.md`.

---

**Refuse the word "interview."** In your head an interview is a test you pass
or fail, and that framing makes people stiff, over-rehearsed, and absent from
the room. What's actually happening with Tasos is a conversation — something
two people build together, not something one performs at the other. He won't
leave with a score. He'll leave with a feeling: *did I enjoy that, can I
picture doing it every week.* That feeling is co-created. That's the lens.

**You're walking in with the wind at your back — play it accordingly.** Tasos
already wants someone like you: he's said he wants a UKG-impacted candidate,
you were first in, you have advocates. When the other person is already
leaning toward you, the job is not to win them — it's to avoid handing them a
reason to hesitate. The most common way people damage a conversation they
were already winning is by over-performing: talking too long, turning warmth
into a pitch, answering and then continuing because silence feels dangerous,
and in that third unnecessary minute planting a doubt. When you're ahead, the
discipline is restraint. Answer, then stop, and let him come back in.

**Prepare topics — including topics about his world.** Topic forethought
isn't faking; it's what frees you to be present instead of scrambling.
Prepare what you want to cover, and prepare genuine curiosity about *his*
side — the CX teams, Certinia, the services-ops reality. Curiosity you've
prepared is still curiosity.

**Ask him questions, follow-ups especially.** People like the people who ask
them more questions, and a follow-up question is the clearest signal that you
were actually listening — you can't fake a good one, it has to be built from
what he just said. Don't save questions for the end. Weave them through.

**Don't boomerask him.** The want will pull you toward using his answers as
runways — "that's interesting, because at UKG I…" The moment you do, you've
taken something he offered and made it about you. Sit in his answer. Be
curious about it for its own sake. Connect it to yourself later, if at all.

**When he tests you, be straight.** He'll probably probe the Salesforce-admin
depth or the gap between this role and where you're really aiming. Don't
deflect. Naming plainly what you don't know reads as trustworthiness; people
extend more trust to someone accurate about their limits than to someone
smooth.

**The hard part: be yourself.** You've been prepped within an inch of your
life. The risk is showing up as a polished performance of a candidate instead
of a person. The boomerang situation is a genuine gift — you can be more
yourself in this room than in any other interview, because you know this
company, this work, these people. Let the preparation recede once you're in
the chair. The most likable version of you is not the most rehearsed one.
It's the one who already feels like he belongs there, because he does.
