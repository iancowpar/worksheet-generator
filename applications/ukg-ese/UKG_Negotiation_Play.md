# UKG offer — negotiation play

Pre-built for the scenario where UKG-PO comes back with an offer.
Built on the decision-framework outcome: $140K base is the green
line; $135-140K is the push-up zone; below $135K is decline.

## The mental model before the offer call

You are a returning boomerang with internal advocacy from Dara and
Steve. You have parallel live processes (Deltek panel Wednesday,
Preservica advancing). You are negotiating from a position of mild
strength, not desperation. Your goal is not to maximize comp — it's
to land at $140K cleanly so you can say yes with confidence.

Three disciplines for the moment the offer lands:

1. **Don't accept verbally.** Take 24-48 hours. The line is below.
2. **Don't negotiate on the call.** Acknowledge, ask for time, take
   the conversation to email.
3. **Don't name your other processes by company.** Reference them as
   "parallel processes" if at all. UKG knows you're interviewing.

## Step 1 — The verbal first response

When the offer is shared verbally (likely by Steve, possibly Dara):

> "Thank you, I really appreciate you and Dara moving this so
> quickly. Can I take a day or two to look at it carefully and come
> back with a formal response?"

That answer:
- Acknowledges the work that got the offer there (Dara's escalation
  was real political capital)
- Doesn't react to the number on the call
- Buys honest thinking time
- Doesn't pressure them

If Steve presses for a reaction in the moment, your honest line:

> "On first read it's a real offer and I'm taking it seriously. The
> specifics I want to think through are the base relative to where
> I've been anchoring, and how the RSUs and bonus structure pull
> through. A day or two of thinking time is genuinely useful to me."

## Step 2 — The formal counter (email, 24-48 hours later)

### If the offer is $140K+ (green line)

You don't need a counter. The play is acceptance with one small move
to lock in clean expectations.

```
Hi Steve [and Dara if appropriate],

Thanks for the offer, and for the speed in getting it through. I'd
like to accept. A few quick logistical items I'd want to confirm
before signing:

- RSU grant details (number of shares, vesting schedule, vest cliff)
- Target bonus % and the structure (company vs. individual gates)
- Start date (I'd target [X])
- Sign-on bonus if there's any flex on that — would help with
  bridging out of my current process

Otherwise, happy to move forward. Looking forward to being back.

Ian
```

The sign-on ask is the soft retreat lever — they may or may not
honor it; not worth dying on the hill. Don't lead with it.

### If the offer is $135-140K (push-up zone)

This is the real negotiation. The play:

```
Hi Steve,

Thanks for the offer and for getting it through quickly — please
pass my thanks to Dara as well. I want to be honest about where I'm
anchoring so we're working from the same picture.

The offer is at $[X]. I've been anchoring my conversations at
$140K base, which sits within the published band and reflects the
scope I'd be bringing in — the AI capability work at UKG, the
boomerang advantage on time-to-productivity, and the parallel
processes I'm balancing.

Is there room within band to land this at $140K? I understand if
it's complicated; I'd rather raise it directly than sit on it. The
RSU grant and bonus structure also matter to me, and if base is
fixed, I'd be open to discussing structure on those.

Happy to chat by phone if useful.

Ian
```

Why this works:
- Names the specific number ($140K) as your anchor rather than asking
  for an undefined increase
- References the published band — you're not asking outside the
  range, you're asking within it
- Surfaces the parallel processes briefly without naming companies
- Opens the door to structural negotiation (RSUs, bonus) if base is
  truly fixed
- Stays warm with the Dara reference
- "Happy to chat by phone" gives them the option to escalate or
  resolve quickly

### If the offer is below $135K

Different play. You're declining at this level, but doing it in a
way that keeps the door open for the Lead PM or Alliances roles via
Dara.

```
Hi Steve,

Thanks for the offer and for moving this so quickly. I want to be
direct with you: at $[X], it doesn't get me where I need to be
financially, and given the parallel processes I'm balancing, I
don't think I can take it at this level.

I'd love to stay in conversation. If there's room to land this
within band closer to $140K, I'm happy to talk further. And if
this particular role doesn't work, I'd want to stay on Dara's
radar for the Lead PM and Alliances roles when those open up —
those have always been the right-shape conversations.

Truly grateful for the advocacy.

Ian
```

That answer:
- Doesn't burn the relationship
- Names the cash floor directly
- Surfaces the Lead/Alliances path explicitly
- Keeps Dara in the conversation

## Step 3 — Handling what comes back

### If they meet $140K+

Accept clean. Move to the logistical email above. Notify Mark
(Preservica) and Scott Kitts (Deltek) when appropriate, with
graceful exit language we'd draft when it's actually time.

### If they hold at the original number

Two-part question:

1. **Has Deltek panel landed yet?** If yes and Deltek looks
   strong, you have alternative leverage. Reply to Steve: *"I
   understand the constraint. Could I have until end of week to
   make a final call? I have one parallel process closing in that
   window."* Buys you space.

2. **Has Deltek not landed yet?** Then decide whether $135-138K is
   enough to take vs. holding for Deltek (uncertain) and
   Preservica (lower priority). Honest read on the trade-off,
   make the call.

### If they come back with a Lead PM frame

Best case. The whole picture shifts. Comp goes up, scope goes up,
the role becomes the answer you'd been hoping for. Take it
graciously and move into formal acceptance.

## What NOT to do at any point

- **Don't reference Preservica or Deltek by name.** "Parallel
  processes" or "I have an offer in another process" is fine. Names
  are not.
- **Don't share the actual Preservica or Deltek band numbers** as
  leverage. Even if true, naming specifics looks transactional and
  could read as a bluff if those numbers are below or near UKG's.
- **Don't escalate to Dara directly without Steve in the loop.**
  Steve's been the channel; running around him damages the
  relationship even if it gets results.
- **Don't say "I really want to come back to UKG."** It's true but
  saying it weakens your negotiation position. The work and the fit
  speak for themselves.
- **Don't accept the lower number with public grumbling about it.**
  Either accept clean or push for more. The middle path of "fine,
  but..." is the worst of both worlds.

## What's worth pre-deciding now

Before the offer call, sit with this question and have an answer:
**at $138K base + RSUs + bonus, do you take it?**

That's the realistic landing point Dara likely pushes for. If you
already know whether $138K is a yes or a "push for $140K then yes,"
the negotiation moment is much easier.

My read on what your answer should be:
- $138K with strong RSU grant (~$30-40K/yr value over vesting) and
  10% bonus = ~$155K total, which is above your green line in
  total-comp terms, not just base. **Likely yes.**
- $138K with weak RSU grant and 5% bonus = ~$145K total. **Push for
  $140K first; if held, decide based on Deltek state.**

You should pre-decide this before the offer call. It's better to
know your own answer than to figure it out under pressure.
