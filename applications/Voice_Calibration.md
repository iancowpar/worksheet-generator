# Ian's voice — calibration notes for drafting

Drawn from the post-screen Steve Marshall follow-up (he rewrote my draft;
his version is the reference). Use these every time we draft anything in
Ian's voice.

## Voice patterns

- **Tighter. Cut adjectives and meta-commentary.** "Great to reconnect this
  morning." beats "Genuinely good to reconnect this morning. Strange and
  good kind of full-circle..." Three words can do the work of two
  sentences.
- **Commas over em-dashes.** He flows clauses with commas; I default to
  em-dashes. Quieter rhythm.
- **Spelled-out numbers in prose.** "Day one" not "day 1." "Week one"
  reads warmer; "week 1" reads transactional.
- **Word choices:** "friction" over "data limitations" (broader, more
  relatable). Watch for this kind of substitution — concrete-but-broad
  beats specific-but-narrow.
- **No meta-commentary.** Cut phrases like "the layer I want to be exact
  about" / "because it's the one I'd most enjoy building for." Ends on
  direct, honest claims instead.
- **Warmth comes through specifics, not claims of warmth.** "Enjoy your
  PTO and the extra-long weekend" lands. "Real thanks for the warmth"
  doesn't. Personal/timing detail > abstract appreciation.
- **No hedge-tails.** Cut "either way," "regardless of how this lands,"
  "in any case." Not his register.
- **Set cadence naturally.** "Hope to hear from you next week" — gentle
  expectation without ask.
- **Include concrete timing when known.** Tuesday meeting, next week,
  Memorial Day weekend, etc.

## Quick checklist before sending any draft in Ian's voice

- [ ] Cut every adjective that isn't load-bearing
- [ ] Em-dashes → commas (default)
- [ ] "day 1" → "day one"
- [ ] Any meta-commentary phrases ("the part I'd most enjoy", etc.) → cut
- [ ] Any "either way" / "regardless" tails → cut
- [ ] Warmth via specific timing/personal details, not abstract claims
- [ ] Forward cadence ("next week," etc.) set naturally if known
- [ ] Read aloud — does it sound like a person who doesn't waste words?
