# EOTSS Senior IT PM — Contract (corrected strategic read)

The posting is a **contract role, not permanent state FTE.** This corrects the
earlier "fixed pay grade / stability / pension play" framing — that applies to
FTE state positions and is wrong for a contract.

## What contract changes

- **No pension, no state benefits, no tenure.** Not a stability play. Do not
  weight it as one.
- **Rate is negotiable.** Commonwealth contract IT PM engagements bill materially
  higher hourly than the FTE-equivalent salary — the premium compensates for no
  benefits/pension/security. There IS comp leverage here: an hourly-rate + term
  negotiation, not a fixed grade.
- **Likely vendor-routed.** Probably a staffing vendor on a Commonwealth contract
  vehicle, not MassCareers direct hire. Gatekeeper may be a vendor recruiter, not
  state HR — adjust the warm channel target accordingly.
- **Finite term, but high contract-to-FTE conversion** in MA state IT. Credible
  foot-in-the-door at EOTSS + real public-sector résumé exposure + a genuine
  conversion path.

## How to weight it in the pipeline

Bridge / cash-flow + public-sector-entry + conversion-optionality play, run in
parallel with the FTE tracks (Deltek, UKG-ESE, Suffolk). Judge on **rate, term,
and conversion likelihood** — not stability. Not a destination; a strong parallel
option with optionality.

## Documents

No rebuild. The résumé and cover letter already lean on rapid ramp, at-risk
stabilization, and structure-from-ambiguity — exactly the contract-stabilizer
profile. Cover-letter mission paragraph stays (a contractor showing mission
alignment differentiates).

## Intel to get BEFORE applying (the real job for the warm channel)

Not "flag my name" — "find out the mechanics":
1. Term length (6 mo? 12? renewable?)
2. Bill rate / rate range, and W2-via-vendor vs. C2C vs. 1099
3. Which vendor / contract vehicle the req sits on
4. Is contract-to-hire explicitly on the table, and typical conversion timeline
5. Who actually screens — vendor recruiter or EOTSS PMO directly

Decision on whether/how hard to pursue depends on 1, 2, and 4.
