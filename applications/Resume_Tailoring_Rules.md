# Resume Tailoring Rules — Audra-Approved

Per the framework from Audra Lenox (Lead TA Partner, UKG, March 2026): https://www.linkedin.com/in/audralenox/

## The master

`applications/Ian_Cowpar_Resume_Master.md` is the single source of truth. All per-role versions derive from it. Read it first.

## The discipline

- **10-20% adjustment per role.** Not 30-50%. If a per-role version diverges from the master by more than 20%, something is wrong — either the master is incomplete or the per-role version is over-engineered.
- **Same tagline across roles** (or near-identical). Master tagline holds.
- **First 6-8 seconds rule.** Lead each top bullet with the load-bearing phrase. Recruiters scan, they don't read.
- **Match JD language where accurate.** Pull repeated JD terms into the matching bullets. Don't invent terms to match.
- **No keyword stuffing. No fabrication. No skills he doesn't have.**

## What to adjust per role

1. **Summary opener** — slide the lead phrase to mirror the JD's framing. Keep core content.
2. **Skills section** — reorder categories to put the most-relevant first. Don't invent new categories per role.
3. **Top 1-2 bullets** — move the most-relevant work up to bullets 1 and 2. Underlying bullets stay the same.

## What NOT to adjust per role

- The tagline (or only minor tuning of the four phrases)
- The bullet phrasing — don't rewrite the same work in new language per role
- The skills category structure — just reorder within it
- The basic voice and tone

## When new receipts surface

Update the **master** first. Don't add to a per-role version without updating the master. The master is always current.

## AI-detection avoidance

Master uses Ian's voice phrases: *"opinionated routing, not a dashboard,"* *"built for signal, not ceremony,"* *"ships working prototypes, not specs,"* *"without a top-down mandate,"* *"the only kind of adoption that lasts."* Preserve these.

Avoid generic AI-resume polish: robust, dynamic, innovative, strategic, leverage (as verb), synergy, "passionate about," "results-driven," "proven track record" (used sparingly is fine; as a default lead it's a tell).

## Process when Ian says "tailor a resume for [role]"

1. Read the JD carefully — note repeated language, ordering of responsibilities, the framing of the hiring manager's vision if available
2. Read the current master at `applications/Ian_Cowpar_Resume_Master.md`
3. Identify the 1-2 master bullets most relevant to the role — those become the top of the experience section
4. Adjust the summary opener (one or two sentences) to mirror the JD framing
5. Reorder skills section categories to lead with what the role values
6. Leave the rest of the master intact
7. Save to `applications/[company]/Ian_Cowpar_Resume_[Company].md`
8. Show Ian the diff against the master — what changed and why — so he can confirm the 20% rule was followed
